# Build Slow, Residualized, Cost-Aware Alpha: 2026 Plan

The best working alpha for a US equity book in 2026 is not a new architecture but a discipline: predict factor- and industry-residualized returns at multi-week horizons with a gradient-boosted-tree plus seed-bagged neural ensemble over a rank-based value/momentum/quality/low-risk core, then protect it with a Barra-style risk model, a square-root cost term inside a convex optimizer, and closing-auction execution. The out-of-sample evidence is sobering and consistent: published anomalies keep roughly half their gross return post-sample and near zero net in liquid names after 2005 ([Chen & Velikov](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225/S0022109022000874a.pdf/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies.pdf)); cost-inclusive ML long-short strategies land at a **net Sharpe of about 0.84** at best in the most careful 2025 study ([Azevedo, Hoegner & Velikov](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2025/program/paper/TihddefB)); the headline LLM news signal decayed from a Sharpe of 6.54 to 1.22 in under three years ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)); and the one leakage-safe audit of LLM-discovered US large-cap strategies found none surviving deflation ([Gençay 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734)). What does transfer from the frontier is structural: residualized targets roughly 2.5x the Sharpe of raw-return targets with the same model ([Guijarro-Ordonez, Pelger & Zanotti](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)), 3–12-month training horizons keep net alpha that 1-month horizons lose ([Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2023/07/the-term-structure-of-machine-learning-alpha)), fine-tuned or embedding-based text models beat prompted sentiment in every head-to-head ([Guo & Hauptmann](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18103v2)), and equity loan fees are the single most persistent non-text predictor ([Engelberg et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v71y2025i7p5529-5551.html)). A part-time engineer should therefore budget most of the first year on data, risk, cost and validation plumbing, treat **net Sharpe 0.5–1.0** as success, and regard any backtest above roughly 2 net as a bug until proven otherwise.

## 1. Executive summary: the evidence sets a net Sharpe 0.5–1.0 target and a cost-first build order

Three independent literatures converge on the same calibration. On factors, 85% of 153 published factors replicate gross in both US and global data under consistent construction ([Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/)), yet the average of 204 anomalies earns about 4 bp per month net after publication and after 2005, with value-weighted implementations at roughly −2 bp per month and only 14 of 204 clearing |t| > 2 net versus about 10 expected by chance ([Chen & Velikov](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225/S0022109022000874a.pdf/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies.pdf)). The strictest post-publication out-of-sample test, which fixes portfolio weights from pre-publication data, finds alphas collapse from 9.9%/yr in-sample to **1.1%/yr (t = 0.15)** out-of-sample, an 89% drop, with 47.5% of anomalies negative ([Gonçalves, Loudis & Ogden](https://finance-conference.wpcarey.asu.edu/sites/default/files/2025-02/Out-of-Sample_Alphas_%282025-02-12%29_with_link.pdf)). On machine learning, the textbook gross result (neural-net long-short decile Sharpe 1.35 value-weighted versus 0.61 for a three-characteristic OLS) is real ([Gu, Kelly & Xiu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398.pdf)), but restricting to published anomalies, post-2005 data and realistic costs cuts ML strategy performance by about 57%, leaving the best model (an LSTM) at net Sharpe 0.84 on two-sided monthly turnover of 122–140% ([Azevedo, Hoegner & Velikov](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2025/program/paper/TihddefB)). On live deployment, 215 bank-sponsored alternative-beta indices showed a **median 73% Sharpe deterioration** from backtest to live, with the most complex strategies losing over 30 percentage points more than the simplest ([Suhonen, Lennkh & Perez via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/?p=36892)).

The practical consequences shape the whole plan. First, alpha must come from combination, cost-aware implementation and horizon matching, not from any single anomaly; Chen and Velikov's combination strategies earned 180–190 bp per month net in 1985–2005 but at most 21 bp per month after 2005, before price impact and borrow ([Chen & Velikov](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225/S0022109022000874a.pdf/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies.pdf)). Second, the binding constraint on a small book is net edge rather than capacity, so all-in costs must stay well under 10 bp per side; the explicit fee stack at Interactive Brokers Pro is only about 2 bp round trip, and half-spread plus impact in liquid large caps under 1% of ADV runs roughly 5–10 bp per side, with small caps at 20 bp or more ([Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f68262/f68262.pdf); [IBKR commissions](https://ndcdyn.interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/commissions-stocks.php)). Third, the risk and portfolio layers determine how much alpha survives: a constrained long-only mandate transfers only about 30% of forecast information into positions, versus 0.7–0.8 for long-short books ([Clarke, de Silva & Thorley via Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btgijdgl1kk3akuoutc0/home/the-cost-of-constraint)), and an optimizer running on a misaligned risk model realized 4.11% active risk against a 3% target ([Saxena & Stubbs](https://www.math.ttu.edu/conferences/past/mcmhr/docs/saxena.pdf)). Fourth, because a true Sharpe of 0.6 needs roughly 11 years of out-of-sample data to reach t ≈ 2 ([Gençay 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734)), go-live decisions must rest on process controls (trial ledgers, deflated Sharpe, locked holdouts, train/live parity) rather than on a short paper-trading window.

The build order follows from the evidence: Phase 0 data and infrastructure; Phase 1 a rank-based factor composite with a working risk model and cost model, which already captures most of the net alpha the literature supports; Phase 2 the ML layer as an incremental signal that must beat Phase 1 net of costs; Phase 3 text and alternative data as conditioning overlays; Phase 4 paper trading with a formal tracking protocol; Phase 5 live with kill switches. Each phase has acceptance criteria in Section 9.

## 2. Alpha model: residualized, multi-week, tree-plus-NN ensembles over a slow factor core

### 2.1 What works out of sample now

**Surviving factor themes.** The themes that survive value-weighting, microcap exclusion and costs are value, momentum, quality/profitability, investment and low-risk; the Jensen–Kelly–Pedersen replication finds 10 of 13 themes above a 75% replication rate, with Seasonality, Leverage and Size the weakest, and post-publication decay of roughly one-third on average ([Alpha Architect on JKP](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/)). Peer review itself confers no protection: mining roughly 29,000 accounting ratios and keeping |t| > 2 yields predictability similar to published predictors, and both decay about 50% post-sample ([Chen, Lopez-Lira & Zimmermann](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10317)). The live record since 2023 is instructive for construction rather than selection: momentum was the standout theme in 2023–2024, then an equity momentum gauge fell 21% in four weeks to March 10, 2025 ([Hedgeweek, March 2025](https://www.hedgeweek.com/hedge-fund-momentum-trades-hit-hard-by-market-volatility/)), and in July 2026 a widely tracked long-short momentum strategy dropped more than 3% in each of two consecutive weeks after positioning hit its highest level since 2002, with Goldman estimating systematic funds at +11.1% year-to-date versus +16% for fundamental managers ([Hedgeweek, July 2026](https://www.hedgeweek.com/quant-funds-suffer-steepest-momentum-drawdown-since-2023-amid-factor-rotation/)). Value led in Q4 2025 (+2.59% versus MSCI ACWI) while Low Volatility lagged (−1.25%), weakest in the US ([Russell Investments Q4 2025](https://russellinvestments.com/content/dam/ri/files/us/en/institutions/articles/Equity-Factor-Investing-Report_2025-Q4.pdf)). Momentum therefore remains the highest-Sharpe single theme but now crashes once or twice a year in the AI-concentrated market, which argues for volatility scaling and crowding controls on that sleeve specifically.

**Factor momentum as the dynamic weighting rule.** Among 65 US factors, 59 have positive one-month autocorrelation and a combined time-series factor-momentum strategy reaches Sharpe 0.84 with a one-month lookback, robust to controls for stock momentum, industry momentum, short-term reversal and transaction costs ([Gupta & Kelly via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-factor-momentum-really-everywhere/)). No post-publication test through 2026 was found, so treat this as the best-supported tilt rule, not a certainty. Volatility scaling, by contrast, should be confined to momentum (and possibly profitability and betting-against-beta): across 103 strategies, real-time volatility-managed versions generally earned lower Sharpe and certainty-equivalent returns than unmanaged, with only 8 consistently better ([Cederburg et al. via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/the-performance-of-volatility-managed-portfolios/)).

**ML on residualized returns.** The gross case for ML is nonlinearity among price-trend, liquidity and volatility features: in the GKX panel (train 1957–74, 30-year OOS test 1987–2016), monthly OOS R² rises from 0.16% for OLS-3 to 0.40% for a three-layer net, and to 0.52–0.70% for trees and nets on the top 1,000 stocks ([Gu, Kelly & Xiu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398.pdf)). The structural lesson that transfers across architectures is to predict residuals: on roughly 550 large liquid US stocks (OOS 2002–2016), the same CNN+Transformer encoder reaches OOS Sharpe 4.16 on IPCA residuals, 3.36 on PCA residuals, 3.21 on Fama-French residuals and only 1.64 on raw returns, with about half the Sharpe persisting at a one-week holding period and gains flattening after about five factors ([Guijarro-Ordonez, Pelger & Zanotti](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)). Those are gross numbers; the post-cost table was not in the retrievable text. The net lesson is about horizon: one-month-horizon ML models had strong gross alphas but net US performance close to zero after 2004, while models trained on 3-, 6- and 12-month targets did considerably better net in 2004–2021 because they trade less and load on quality and value ([Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2023/07/the-term-structure-of-machine-learning-alpha)). Among cost-mitigation techniques, only a two-month holding period marginally improved net returns ([Quantpedia on Azevedo et al.](https://quantpedia.com/the-expected-returns-of-machine-learning-strategies/)).

**Model families.** In the most recent large-scale US benchmark (about 10,000 securities, 2001–2023, expanding window, yearly retraining), CatBoost remained the strongest baseline on statistical fit, with a long-short Sharpe of 6.79 before costs that should be read as a calibration ceiling, not a target ([Rahimikia, Ni & Wang](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)). In the 2025 competition record, gradient-boosted trees remain the default tabular winner, usually ensembled with neural nets such as TabM ([ML Contests 2025](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-machine-learning-competitions-2025)); the top Jane Street 2021 solution blended an autoencoder-augmented MLP with XGBoost, each averaged over three seeds, under purged group time-series CV ([Jane Street 2021 summary](https://www.besthub.dev/articles/kaggle-jane-street-market-prediction-competition-summary-and-model-insights-accb0dc2927c)). Deep cross-sectional transformers win on specific setups but are fragile: MASTER reports IC 0.064 on CSI300 in its own split ([MASTER](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15235)), yet an independent re-run on a different period gives it IC 0.0215 versus LightGBM 0.0277 ([R&D-Agent(Q)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15155v2)), and Qlib's own leaderboard shows information-ratio standard deviations across 20 seeds of ±0.2–0.6, so top-model rankings are not statistically separable ([Qlib benchmarks](https://github.com/microsoft/qlib/blob/main/examples/benchmarks/README.md)).

**Text and LLM signals.** The best-documented text signal is next-day drift after firm-specific headlines: on 159,137 US firm-headline-days from October 2021 to May 2024 (post-cutoff for GPT-4-0314), the tradable drift has hit rates of 58%/55%, pre-cost Sharpe 2.97 overnight, roughly five times stronger in small firms, profitable at 10 bp round trip (Sharpe 1.29, or 1.34 with 25% partial rebalancing that cuts turnover from about 190% to 46% per day) and unprofitable at 20 bp, with annualized Sharpe falling from 6.54 in 2021Q4 to 1.22 in January–May 2024 ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)). The method ranking is consistent across independent papers: LoRA fine-tuning of Mistral-7B/Llama3-8B directly to monthly returns gives a North American long-short Sharpe of 1.49 versus 0.92 for FinBERT sentiment and 0.39 for FinVader, with mean-pooled token embeddings beating bottleneck tokens ([Guo & Hauptmann](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18103v2)); structured event triplets extracted by GPT-3.5 feeding an attention model earn 10.93% annualized (t = 3.31, Sharpe 0.78) OOS 2008–2022, while a one-period execution delay flips returns negative ([Li, Qiao & Zheng](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.19484)); and the Chen–Kelly–Xiu embedding-plus-ridge design finds larger LLMs and ensembles of LLMs do best ([Kelly Wharton slides](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Kelly-WhartonJL.pdf)), though its OOS Sharpe tables could not be retrieved and should not be quoted. For a large-cap book the most relevant result is text as a conditioning overlay: GPT-4o-mini news scores filtering an S&P 500 12-1 momentum portfolio raised post-cutoff OOS Sharpe from 0.79 to 1.06 net of 2 bp over a 15-month window, with alpha significant only at the 10% level and a basic prompt beating an elaborate one ([Anic et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26228v1)).

**Alternative data.** Equity loan fees deliver the highest monthly long-short return (4.01%) and monthly Sharpe (0.66) among 103 strategies, persistent through the sample, with 42% of the outperformance unexplained by other anomalies ([Engelberg et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v71y2025i7p5529-5551.html)). Options-implied volatility spread and skew predict returns largely because implied vols embed borrow fees; excluding high-fee stocks cuts their predictability by at least two-thirds, so they are not an independent factor once borrow exposure is neutralized ([Muravyev, Pearson & Pollet](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v172y2025ics0304405x25001618.html)). Similarweb web traffic for 1,000+ large US firms forecasts quarterly results and earns risk-adjusted long-short returns, concentrated in transactional sites and retail-owned names ([Berkeley Haas on Armstrong et al.](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/companies-website-traffic-proves-powerful-predictor-of-financial-performance-and-stock-returns/)); magnitudes were not in the available brief.

### 2.2 What does not work, with the evidence

| Approach | Verdict | Key evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| End-to-end LLM trading agents (FinMem, TradingAgents, QuantAgent, FinAgent) | Reject as portfolio managers; use only as upstream extractors | Post-cutoff reproduction: TradingAgents Sharpe 0.43 gross → 0.22 net, below buy-and-hold on 4 of 5 stocks; QuantAgent −0.96 → −1.15; standard errors ≈ reported Sharpes | [Alpha Illusion](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.16895v1); [FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v3) |
| Zero-shot time-series foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM) | Exclude or use as feature generators only | Chronos-large R² −1.37%; TimesFM-500M R² −2.80%, accuracy < 50%; fine-tuning mostly made them worse; CatBoost remained best | [Rahimikia et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1) |
| Finance-pretrained TSFM (Kronos) | Unproven | Only relative gains published (RankIC +93% vs leading TSFM); no absolute IC, Sharpe or post-cost results | [Kronos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02739) |
| Deep RL for portfolio weights | Reject for allocation; confine to low-dimensional meta-decisions | Most DRL algorithms not robust, generalize poorly, degrade quickly; policy instability across seeds acknowledged by the FinRL contest organizers | [Velay et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2306.10950); [FinRL Contests](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02281v3) |
| Naive end-to-end Sharpe-maximizing networks at S&P 500 scale | Reject | E2E trailed predict-then-optimize in all six S&P 500 configs and 5 of 6 NASDAQ-100 configs, often with negative Sharpe | [Kim et al., ICAIF'25](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13544v3) |
| LLM-mined formulaic alphas as deployed strategies | Feature-generation accelerator at most | Best IS Sharpe 1.69 → OOS 0.18; DSR 0.86 below the 0.95 bar; Claude search IS 0.44 → OOS −0.33; S&P 500 AlphaAgent IC 0.0056 with sell-side fees only | [Gençay](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734); [AlphaAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789v2) |
| "Virtue of complexity" (P ≫ T random-feature models) | Audit before trusting | With short windows the forecast collapses to a recency-weighted average, i.e., volatility-timed momentum regardless of data; weak signals need 25–30+ years | [Nagel](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34104); [Fallahgoul](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03780v3) |
| Standalone 1–20-day signals (short-term reversal, 1-month ML) | Execution-timing overlays only | 1-month ML net ≈ 0 post-2004; reversal standalone has extremely high turnover; Dimensional uses it to delay trades | [Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2023/07/the-term-structure-of-machine-learning-alpha); [Dimensional](https://www.dimensional.com/us-en/insights/q-and-a-on-short-run-reversals-with-mamdouh-medhat-and-robert-novy-marx) |
| PEAD in non-microcaps | Dead since ~2006 | t = 2.18 all stocks, 1.43 ex-microcaps, 2001–2024; "revival" papers do not filter microcaps | [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again/); [Martineau](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/CFR-0122) |
| Retail order imbalance (BJZZ) | Decayed | No longer predicts weekly large-cap returns 2016–2021; long-short no longer profitable | [Ardia, Aymard & Cenesizoglu](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2403.17095) |
| LLM numeric forecasting from price histories | Reject | GPT-4o over-extrapolates recent returns (≈38 vs ≈12 for humans) and the extrapolative component is negatively related to next-week returns | [arXiv 2409.11540](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.11540) |
| ML profits in microcaps/distressed names | Not implementable | Deep-learning predictability concentrates in hard-to-arbitrage stocks; excluding microcaps, distressed firms or high-vol periods considerably weakens it | [Avramov, Cheng & Metzker](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3450322) |

Two further cautions apply to anything LLM-based. Training-data contamination is measurable: for Llama-3.3-70B the headline signal's marginal effect is 0.30% where the model has memorized the outcome versus 0.14% where it has not (interaction t = 3.64), vanishing post-cutoff ([Lookahead Propensity test](https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.23847.md)); twelve 1.3B models pretrained from scratch with yearly cutoffs show a lookahead premium of 26.4 bp per standard deviation (t = 10.65) for instruct models ([DatedGPT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11838)); chronologically trained encoders judge the bias modest in a ridge-regression setting ([ChronoBERT](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21206v3)). The reconciliation is that contamination is worst for large instruction-tuned models queried with identifiable firm-date pairs, and smallest for embedding pipelines with small encoders. Crowding is the other: off-the-shelf models add little meta-model contribution at Numerai because everyone runs them, and a preprocessing mismatch between backtest and live caused inconsistent live results for a top-10 participant ([Numerai 2025 write-up](https://kenfus.github.io/machine%20learning/quant/competition/2025/06/20/numerai-2025.html)).

### 2.3 Recommended alpha architecture

| Component | Specification | Rationale and source |
|---|---|---|
| Universe | Top ~1,000–1,500 US stocks by market cap and ADV; exclude bottom NYSE size quintile by construction; price ≥ $5; point-in-time membership including delisted names | Microcaps are ≈3% of market value and where spurious alpha lives ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again/)); ML profits concentrate there ([Avramov et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3450322)); delisted names and PIT constituents are required for honest tests ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v3)) |
| Feature set (Tier 1, Phase 1) | ~60–100 features: value (B/P, E/P, S/EV, CF/P within industry), momentum (12-1, 6-1, industry momentum, momentum change), quality/profitability (GP/A, ROE, accruals, asset growth), low-risk (beta, idio vol, max return), liquidity (turnover, Amihud, dollar volume, spread), short interest and borrow fee, plus 1-month residual reversal as a timing feature | GKX importance ordering: price trends, then liquidity, then risk ([GKX](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398.pdf)); loan fees as a standard predictor ([Engelberg et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v71y2025i7p5529-5551.html)); OSAP provides 209 firm-level signals free ([openassetpricing](https://pypi.org/project/openassetpricing/)) |
| Feature set (Tier 2, Phase 2) | Add Alpha158/Alpha360-style price-volume features computed on US data, factor-residual return sequences (30–60-day lookback), realized-vol features | Qlib feature sets as reference ([Qlib](https://github.com/microsoft/qlib/blob/main/examples/benchmarks/README.md)); DLSA uses 30-day residual lookback ([DLSA](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)) |
| Feature processing | Per-date winsorize at 1st/99th percentile, cross-sectional rank or z-score, industry (GICS or FF-48) demean for value/quality/investment; identical code path in training and live | Era-wise processing and process parity ([Numerai 2025](https://kenfus.github.io/machine%20learning/quant/competition/2025/06/20/numerai-2025.html)); JKP's consistent construction lifts replication above 80% ([Alpha Architect on JKP](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/)) |
| Targets | Primary: 20-trading-day forward return residualized against the Phase-1 risk model's market, industry and style factors, then cross-sectionally z-scored per date. Secondary heads: 5-day and 60-day residual returns | Residualized targets 2.5x Sharpe of raw ([DLSA](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.04028)); 5-day z-scored labels in MASTER ([MASTER](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15235)); 3–12-month horizons retain net alpha ([Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-uk/insights/2023/07/the-term-structure-of-machine-learning-alpha)) |
| Model families | Baseline A: linear rank composite with fixed theme weights. Baseline B: ridge/elastic-net on Tier-1 features. ML: LightGBM and CatBoost (3–5 seeds each) plus a 3-layer MLP (5 seeds, L1, early stopping, batch norm); optional GRU over residual sequences | GBDT + NN ensembles dominate competitions ([ML Contests 2025](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-machine-learning-competitions-2025)); GKX regularization recipe ([GKX](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398.pdf)) |
| Ensembling | Average seed predictions within family; rank-average across families; weights fixed ex ante (not fitted) until Phase 2 evidence justifies stacking | Seed averaging reduces variance ([GKX](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398.pdf)); 1→64 ensemble members raised median Sharpe and narrowed IQR ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13544v3)) |
| Loss | Pointwise MSE on z-scored residual target as primary; rank-correlation loss as a second head; compare under identical CV | Rank-aware losses used by top Numerai participants ([Numerai 2025](https://kenfus.github.io/machine%20learning/quant/competition/2025/06/20/numerai-2025.html)); listwise-vs-pointwise winners not extractable from the 2025 ranking-loss paper |

Expected magnitudes anchor the acceptance criteria. On A-shares a good daily model reaches IC 0.04–0.06 pre-cost ([Qlib benchmarks](https://github.com/microsoft/qlib/blob/main/examples/benchmarks/README.md)); US large caps run 2–4x lower, with NASDAQ-100 IC 0.0162 OOS 2024–mid-2025 ([R&D-Agent(Q)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15155v2)) and S&P 500 IC 0.0056 ([AlphaAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789v2)). At monthly horizons the Grinold convention treats IC 0.05 as good and 0.10 as very good ([MSCI, Converting Scores into Alphas](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/1645561/PI_Converting_Scores_Into_Alphas.pdf/7adf1f42-10aa-40eb-9e8c-ecc11eeba2d4)). A 20-day residualized composite with rank IC around 0.03–0.05 on a 1,000-name universe, rebalanced with partial trading, is the realistic working target.

### 2.4 Research protocol with hurdle rates

Every candidate signal or model passes a fixed gauntlet, logged in a trial ledger that records the number of configurations tried so the deflated Sharpe ratio can be computed honestly ([Gençay](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734)). The protocol has five gates. Gate 1, economic prior: a one-paragraph hypothesis written before the backtest, with the expected sign and horizon; the Chen–Lopez-Lira–Zimmermann finding that theory does not slow decay means this is a discipline against snooping, not a performance predictor ([CLZ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10317)). Gate 2, gross statistics on the ex-microcap, value-weighted, post-2005 sample: mean rank IC and ICIR over purged walk-forward folds, decile spread t-stat with the Harvey–Liu–Zhu threshold of **t > 3.0** rather than 1.96 ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu via CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/taming-the-factor-zoo/)), and incremental IC after regressing on the existing composite (a signal that adds nothing orthogonal is rejected regardless of standalone strength). Gate 3, net statistics: the signal is added to the live composite and the full optimizer-plus-cost pipeline is re-run; acceptance requires a net Sharpe improvement that survives in at least two-thirds of walk-forward folds, following the plateau-and-majority rule ([AlgoXpert protocol](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09219)), with turnover-adjusted breakeven cost reported. Gate 4, robustness: results on equal- and value-weighted versions, by size tercile, by year, and by regime (high/low VIX); for any high-capacity model, a synthetic-data test to check it has not collapsed into a recency-weighted momentum strategy ([Nagel](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34104)). Gate 5, deflation: DSR > 0.95 given the ledger's trial count and PBO < 0.5 via combinatorial purged CV; the planted-oracle result that DSR 1.00 does not detect leakage ([Gençay](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734)) means leakage must be prevented structurally (PIT data, registry-validated features), not detected statistically.

## 3. Risk model: Barra-style fundamental model with nonlinear-shrunk residuals and an alignment factor

The production standard remains a fundamental model: Axioma calls fundamental risk models by far the dominant class and recommends a statistical model alongside at the same horizon to catch transient factors ([SimCorp/Axioma](https://www.simcorp.com/resources/insights/white-papers-and-reports/statistical-versus-fundamental-models)). The architecture to replicate is USE4: a Country factor with unit exposure for every stock, 60 GICS industries with cap-weighted industry returns constrained to sum to zero, 12 style factors (Beta, Non-Linear Beta, Liquidity, Residual Volatility, Momentum, Size, Non-linear Size, Book-to-Price, Growth, Leverage, Dividend Yield, Earnings Yield), factor returns estimated by WLS with weights proportional to √cap, styles standardized to cap-weighted mean 0 and equal-weighted SD 1, and outliers trimmed at 3 SD ([USE4 Methodology Notes](https://www.top1000funds.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/09/USE4_Methodology_Notes_August_2011.pdf)). For a daily-rebalanced book the relevant commercial analog is MSCI's Trading model, which adds eight medium-term styles including Short-term Reversal, 1-Day Reversal, Short Interest, Industry Momentum and Sentiment, plus an implied-volatility adjustment ([MSCI factsheet](https://www.msci.com/documents/1296102/1636401/MSCI_Barra_Market+Equity+Models_Factsheet+.pdf)); a home-built model should include short-term reversal, 1-day reversal and short interest because they carry significant short-horizon variance. The exposure-and-regression layer is a solved engineering problem: toraniko reproduces Barra-like factor returns from daily returns, sector one-hots and a handful of fundamental ratios, estimating 10+ years of daily factor returns in under a minute, though the fetched repository is a fork with no visible recent activity ([toraniko](https://github.com/Pleometric/toraniko)).

The covariance layer is where the 2022–2026 research changes the recipe. Nonlinear shrinkage clearly beats linear once N and T both exceed about 50 and eigenvalues are dispersed, the analytical formula scales to N = 10,000+, and the overall best estimator in Ledoit and Wolf's study is the factor model with DCC-nonlinear-shrunk residuals (AFM-DCC-NL), which works about as well with only the market factor as with all five Fama-French factors provided the market factor is included ([Ledoit & Wolf 2022](https://www.econ.uzh.ch/dam/jcr:e946b1e3-35e8-4c4f-894f-5f4306bf28a5/jfec_2022.pdf)). Deep covariance models have not displaced this: a six-layer transformer eigenvalue shrinker beat Ledoit–Wolf in 72.1% of 261 rolling windows on 50 S&P 500 stocks ([Yang, Zhao & Chai](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.15597)), but in a minimum-variance test at daily rebalancing DCC-NL had the lowest variance (0.002048) versus a CNN-BiLSTM-attention model (0.002293), and the deep model still ends with a shrinkage step ([Reis, Serra & Gama](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01581v1)). With intraday data, a plain HAR model on realized covariance (4.322% annualized OOS risk on 200 S&P stocks) edged out LASSO-based extensions ([Kim & Oh](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.13511)), so realized-covariance methods are a Phase-2+ option only if TAQ-grade data is bought.

The alignment fix is non-negotiable because the problem is created by the optimizer, not the estimator. Alpha typically contains a component orthogonal to the risk factors; the optimizer sees no systematic risk there and loads on it, so at a 3% active-risk target optimized portfolios realized 4.11% with a bias statistic of 1.37 against a 95% band of [0.88, 1.12]; augmenting the covariance with the normalized orthogonal component of implied alpha brought the bias statistic to 0.93, kept the problem convex, and shifted the realized frontier up by 60–80 bp ([Saxena & Stubbs](https://www.math.ttu.edu/conferences/past/mcmhr/docs/saxena.pdf)). SEI's 2026 backtests show the same mechanism from the other side: swapping generic style factors for the firm's own alpha factors and matching half-lives to signal horizon cut Low-Vol turnover from 31–45% per quarter to 15% and flipped net-of-cost outperformance from negative to +0.83%, with an estimated 5–20% risk-adjusted improvement per customization ([SEI](https://www.seic.com/sites/default/files/2026-03/Risk%20Modelling%20of%20Optimized%20Factor%20Portfolios.pdf)). The specification for this project is therefore: include the Phase-1 alpha themes as risk-model styles with horizon-matched half-lives, add an alpha alignment factor built from the orthogonal component of the final composite, and monitor 1 − R² of alpha regressed on risk exposures as the misalignment gauge.

Evaluation uses the bias statistic (std of standardized returns, ideal 1, 95% band ≈ 1 ± 2/√(2T), rolling-12-month 5th/95th percentiles ≈ 0.66/1.34, MRAD ideal 0.17 rising to about 0.19 under kurtosis 3.5–4) on pure-factor portfolios, random long-short portfolios and, decisively, the actual optimized book ([USE4](https://www.top1000funds.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/09/USE4_Methodology_Notes_August_2011.pdf)); QLIKE and Mincer–Zarnowitz on per-stock specific-vol forecasts, reporting multiple losses because rankings differ by loss ([Pollok](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07928)); and OOS minimum-variance portfolio volatility and turnover against Ledoit–Wolf and DCC-NL baselines split by regime, with Diebold–Mariano tests ([Reis et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01581v1)). A factor-crowding monitor can be approximated with public data following MSCI's design (valuation spreads, short-interest spread, pairwise correlation, factor volatility, trailing performance; hedge-fund 13F weight multiples with a 45-day lag), given MSCI's claim that crowded factors show more frequent large drawdowns afterward ([MSCI crowding](https://www.msci.com/research-and-insights/blog-post/eyeing-the-crowds-from-multiple-perspectives)). skfolio ships Ledoit–Wolf, denoising, detoning, EWMA, graphical lasso and implied-covariance estimators plus CVaR/CDaR measures and purged CV, making it the natural evaluation harness ([skfolio](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04176)).

## 4. Transaction cost model: square-root impact plus spread, borrow and 2026 fees in convex form

The square-root law is the consensus pre-trade impact model and has been re-confirmed twice recently. Account-resolved data for all liquid Tokyo stocks over eight years gives an impact exponent of 1/2 within statistical error per stock and per trader ([Sato & Kanazawa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13965v2)); reconstructed AAPL metaorders from Nasdaq ITCH data (178 days, December 2024–August 2025) give a prefactor c_raw = 0.69 and c_eff = 0.34 after bias correction in I/σ_D = c·(Q/V_D)^½, with square-root preferred over linear (ΔAIC = 22) and the prefactor stable across 32 weekly walk-forward recalibrations ([Vasaikar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24019)). Because impact of a metaorder is approximately independent of how many child orders it is split into and over what time, schedule matters little at modest participation ([Bouchaud](https://bouchaud.substack.com/p/the-square-root-law-of-market-impact)). Live-fill regressions agree on the dominant scale factor: AQR's 5.3 million orders (1998–2011, $721B) show mean impact versus arrival of 12.18 bp (large caps 11.21, small caps 21.27), implementation shortfall 13 bp, long-short trades around 10 bp versus long-only around 16 bp, about 70% of impact permanent, and only 3.7 bp measured against VWAP ([Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f68262/f68262.pdf)); Citigroup's data fit temporary impact with exponent 0.600 ± 0.038 on participation rate, found volatility the most important scale factor, and dropped bid-ask spread for lack of consistent effect ([Almgren et al.](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/costestim.pdf)). Buy-side 2022 data show the biggest cost step is from under 5% ADV (−14.62 bp) to 5–10% ADV (−24.6 bp), with high-volatility names at −22.9 bp versus −9.2 bp for low-volatility ([Bloomberg BTCA](https://www.bloomberg.com/professional/?p=46500)).

The functional form for the optimizer is the integral of square-root impact, already implemented in cvxportfolio as **a|x| + b·σ·|x|^{3/2}/V^{1/2} + c·x**, where a is the proportional cost such as half the bid-ask spread, b is typically of order 1, σ is recent return volatility and V is period volume (realized in simulation, forecast in optimization), with defaults of 0.5 cents per share, b = 1.0 and exponent 1.5 ([cvxportfolio costs](https://www.cvxportfolio.com/en/stable/costs.html)); MOSEK documents the same 3/2-power term as a power-cone constraint ([MOSEK cookbook](https://docs.mosek.com/portfolio-cookbook/transaction.html)). Holding costs enter separately: a borrow fee on post-trade short positions ([Boyd et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.00109)), where cvxportfolio's 5%-per-year default is explicitly a rough, optimistic figure for liquid names ([cvxportfolio costs](https://www.cvxportfolio.com/en/stable/costs.html)) and IBKR's actual cost is the borrow fee minus interest on proceeds, varying between General Collateral and Hard-to-Borrow with no published numeric schedule ([IBKR short sale cost](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/short-sale-cost.php?menu=A)). Ledoit and Wolf argue that handling stock-specific costs at the selection stage, rather than deducting a uniform cost afterward, often improves net Sharpe for high-turnover strategies ([Ledoit & Wolf WP 420](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:zur:econwp:420)).

Calibration is a monthly batch job rather than a model: log every parent order with decision price, arrival mid, fills, participation, σ and ADV; regress realized arrival slippage minus half-spread on σ·√(Q/V) pooled across names; update b with shrinkage toward the prior. Nonparametric and cross-impact propagator models slightly beat parametric forms on CFM metaorder data ([Hey, Neuman & Tuschmann](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06879)) but need tens of thousands of metaorders; for a small fund, ML effort is better spent forecasting the inputs σ and V̂ than the impact function.

## 5. Portfolio construction: a single-period convex optimizer tuned to behave like Gârleanu–Pedersen

The baseline is Boyd's single-period optimization: maximize forecast return on trades minus γ_trade × transaction cost, γ_hold × holding cost and γ_risk × factor-model risk, subject to trades netting to zero and a constraint menu covering leverage, per-asset limits relative to capitalization or ADV, minimum cash, beta and factor neutrality, turnover limits, trade-size limits relative to volume and no-trade lists, with any hard constraint convertible to a soft penalty; with a factor covariance it scales as O(nk²) and solves in under half a second for 1,500 assets and 50 factors on one core ([Boyd et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.00109)). Alpha enters via Grinold's rule, α = IC × residual volatility × standardized score, with missing scores set to zero and IC chosen by strategy and regime; unconverted raw scores get eaten by the optimizer ([MSCI, Converting Scores into Alphas](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/1645561/PI_Converting_Scores_Into_Alphas.pdf/7adf1f42-10aa-40eb-9e8c-ecc11eeba2d4)). Expected-return estimation error remains the dominant failure mode: 14 sample-based mean-variance models failed to consistently beat 1/N, with roughly 3,000 months of data needed for 25 assets ([DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v22y2009i5p1915-1953.html)), which argues for scores shrunk heavily toward zero and for an explicit uncertainty set on the mean, not for abandoning optimization.

Gârleanu–Pedersen supplies the multi-signal logic: the aim portfolio is a weighted average of current and expected future Markowitz portfolios, the optimal trade moves a fixed fraction of the gap toward the aim, and each factor is shrunk by its mean-reversion speed so fast signals get less weight as costs rise; their in-sample futures test shows the dynamic rule at net Sharpe 0.58 versus 0.46 for the best static rule and −9.84 for naive Markowitz rebalancing every period ([Gârleanu & Pedersen](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/60191441/lasse_heje_pedersen_et_al_dynamic_trading_acceptedmanuscript.pdf)). Boyd's multi-period variant plans H periods and executes the first, with cost roughly linear in H ([Boyd et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.00109)), but the cvxportfolio documentation gives no guidance on when MPO beats SPO ([cvxportfolio policies](https://www.cvxportfolio.com/en/stable/optimization_policies.html)), and no credible OOS single-stock equity test of the aim rule was found. The practical recommendation is SPO with a turnover penalty and GP-style half-life shrinkage applied in alpha space, which produces nearly the same trajectory; reserve MPO for known term structure such as earnings dates and index rebalances.

| Book type | Dollar-neutral long-short (or 130/30 if borrow is constrained); long-only cap-neutral only as a fallback | TC ≈ 0.7–0.8 L/S vs ≈ 0.3 constrained long-only ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btgijdgl1kk3akuoutc0/home/the-cost-of-constraint)) |

| Soft constraints | Turnover penalty tuned by walk-forward to produce 20–40% of the frictionless trade per day; daily rebalance, partial trade | GP partial trading; 25% partial rebalancing cut turnover 4x with no Sharpe loss ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)) |

| Diagnostics per backtest | Gross/net Sharpe, turnover, transfer coefficient, realized/ex-ante risk ratio, factor attribution, TC over time | [SEI](https://www.seic.com/sites/default/files/2026-03/Risk%20Modelling%20of%20Optimized%20Factor%20Portfolios.pdf); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btgijdgl1kk3akuoutc0/home/the-cost-of-constraint) |

Several approaches should be avoided or deferred. End-to-end decision-focused learning wins in small-universe studies (7 ETFs, Sharpe 1.16 versus 0.79 for risk parity, but with no transaction costs and a single window ([Uysal, Li & Mulvey](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.04636)); 1.33 versus 1.24 at 20 bp costs on 7 ETFs, single split, simplex constraints only ([IPMO](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11273v2))), yet the most practitioner-grounded study found raw E2E Sharpe maximization unstable and inferior to predict-then-optimize on S&P 500 universes, with a supervised "imitate the ex-post optimal weights" head plus 100-run ensembles doing better ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13544v3)). If decision-focused calibration is used at all, limit it to a few scalars (risk aversion, shrinkage intensity, robustness radius). Hierarchical risk parity variants have only synthetic evidence in 2026, where CRISP reached 80–94% of oracle Sharpe but the Schur-complement allocator was unstable at intermediate γ ([Wuebben](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23833v1)); use HRP/NCO as sanity baselines from skfolio, not as the production allocator. Avoid mixed portfolios (optimizing each signal separately and averaging) when signals are correlated; combine in forecast space first. Keep reinforcement learning out of allocation entirely given the robustness record ([Velay et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2306.10950)).

## 6. Execution model: closing-auction default, IBKR algos for the rest, and a TCA loop that calibrates b

For a daily rebalancer the closing auction is the lowest-impact venue per dollar: it carries about 9% of total US daily volume on a typical day (roughly $50B notional), about 20% on index-rebalance and expiration days and over 34% on Russell reconstitution day, with auction prices stable and no outsized deviation at the next open despite late D-order insertions on NYSE ([BMLL](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/into-the-close-unpacking-u-s-closing-auction-dynamics-and-the-impact-of-the-russell-reconstitution)). NYSE MOC/LOC orders can be entered, modified or cancelled until 3:50 pm, after which only contra-side orders against a published significant imbalance are accepted, and imbalance data publishes at 3:50 and then every second when changed ([NYSE closing process](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/NYSE_Auctions_Closing_Process_Fact_Sheet.pdf)). Momentum returns accrue overnight while most other anomalies earn intraday, a pattern stronger in large caps ([Lou, Polk & Skouras](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/cm/publications/discussion-papers/tug-war-overnight-versus-intraday-expected-returns)), which fits a close-of-day entry for momentum longs. Interactive Brokers exposes Arrival Price (maxPctVol 0.1–0.5, risk-aversion settings), best-efforts VWAP, TWAP, POV 1–50% with a no-take-liquidity option, Close Price, Dark Ice, Accumulate/Distribute, Adaptive and a Midprice order type via API; the GitHub reference is marked deprecated in favor of IBKR Campus, so parameters must be verified there before coding ([IBKR algos](https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/ibalgos.html)). A daily MOC rebalancer must be on IBKR Pro, because IBKR Lite charges for OnClose orders beyond 10% of monthly volume ([IBKR commissions](https://ndcdyn.interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/commissions-stocks.php)).

| Order situation | Default tactic | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Order ≤ ~1–2% of the name's typical closing-auction volume | LOC a few bp through the latest indicative/last price, submitted before the 15:50 NYSE cutoff (the 15:55 indicative is published after the cutoff and cannot be used to set the limit) | Caps dislocation risk; auction stability ([BMLL](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/into-the-close-unpacking-u-s-closing-auction-dynamics-and-the-impact-of-the-russell-reconstitution); [NYSE](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/NYSE_Auctions_Closing_Process_Fact_Sheet.pdf)) |
| Larger or less liquid orders | IBKR Close Price or VWAP (noTakeLiq when urgency is low) over the last 1–3 hours, maxPctVol ≤ 5–10% | Impact step above 5% ADV ([Bloomberg BTCA](https://www.bloomberg.com/professional/?p=46500)) |
| Short-horizon alpha (text/event signals) | Arrival Price or Adaptive at the next open; measure delay cost | One-period delay flips event-signal returns negative ([Li, Qiao & Zheng](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.19484)) |
| Reversal-leg trades | Passive/limit execution, never marketable | Reversal is a liquidity-provision return ([Dimensional](https://www.dimensional.com/us-en/insights/q-and-a-on-short-run-reversals-with-mamdouh-medhat-and-robert-novy-marx)) |
| Published imbalance on the same side | Do not add; consider contra-side only if alpha is not time-critical | [NYSE](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/NYSE_Auctions_Closing_Process_Fact_Sheet.pdf) |

The TCA loop is the part of execution that compounds. Store the decision price at signal time, the arrival mid at submission, every fill, the close and the next-day open; decompose each order into delay (arrival − decision), impact (fill VWAP − arrival), reversion (next close − fill), opportunity (unfilled × (close − decision)) and fees; aggregate by %ADV and volatility bucket as Bloomberg's buy-side reporting does ([Bloomberg BTCA](https://www.bloomberg.com/professional/?p=46500)); and normalize by σ·√(Q/V) to update the optimizer's b monthly. Fix the benchmark before comparing tactics, because identical AQR trades measure 12.18 bp against arrival but 3.7 bp against VWAP ([Frazzini et al.](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f68262/f68262.pdf)); for auction fills the relevant benchmark is the 3:50 pm mid or the pre-imbalance mid. Any in-house tactic earns its place only through randomized A/B tests against the broker algo on arrival slippage.

Reinforcement learning for execution is where the simulation-only caveat bites hardest. PPO agents adjusting participation minute by minute reached 2.13 bp arrival slippage versus 7.01 bp for TWAP across 4,900 OOS orders, but inside a simulator with calibrated transient impact, and the authors state the results are not predictions of live execution ([De Witt & Pakkanen](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22113v1)); the DQN study in ABIDES does not test Almgren–Chriss or VWAP ([Hafsi & Vittori](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06389)); the BTC liquidation study spans one OOS month ([RL-Exec](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07434v1)). Because square-root impact is nearly schedule-independent at modest participation ([Bouchaud](https://bouchaud.substack.com/p/the-square-root-law-of-market-impact)), the room for clever scheduling is bounded, and the RL gains come precisely from exploiting simulator structure least likely to transfer. ML in execution earns its keep in two narrower jobs: choosing urgency per order from signal decay, and forecasting intraday volume curves and closing-auction volume. A full limit-order-book simulator is unnecessary for an auction-centric system; a daily-bar backtester with the cvxportfolio-style cost model using realized volume suffices, with NautilusTrader's configurable fill models (probabilistic limit fills, L1 slippage, custom hooks) as the event-driven layer if intraday algos are added later ([NautilusTrader fill models](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/nightly/concepts/backtesting/fill-models/)). Databento publishes a worked example for consuming auction-imbalance messages, which is the starting point for imbalance-aware LOC logic ([Databento](https://databento.com/docs/examples/equities/auction-imbalance)).

## 8. Validation protocol: structural leakage prevention, purged CV, deflated Sharpe, one locked holdout

The validation protocol is the same for every layer and is enforced by code rather than convention. Features are built strictly point-in-time from the Parquet store, with filing-date lags for any current-view fundamentals and a 90-day conservative lag where filing dates are missing. Cross-validation is purged, embargoed walk-forward with state reset, because naive temporal splits leak through indicator lookback and position state; the AlgoXpert protocol uses a 5-day purge gap, selects parameters from the plateau of configurations within 90% of the optimum rather than the peak, passes a configuration only if at least two-thirds of folds meet the benchmark, treats a catastrophic drawdown as an immediate fail, and locks parameters before a single strict holdout ([AlgoXpert](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09219)). Combinatorial purged CV from skfolio produces a distribution of OOS Sharpe rather than one number ([skfolio](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04176)). Every experiment writes to a trial ledger so the deflated Sharpe ratio reflects the real search; a planted look-ahead oracle scored DSR 1.00, so leakage must be made inexpressible upstream, by computing features through registry-validated functions that cannot see future rows ([Gençay](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27734)). For LLM components the ledger also records the model's training cutoff, all evaluation is post-cutoff, and the lookahead-propensity diagnostic is run on the exact prompt before any in-sample number is trusted ([LAP](https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.23847.md)).

Statistical hurdles are set by the base rates. A new factor needs t > 3.0 ([HLZ](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/taming-the-factor-zoo/)); any complex model is tested on synthetic data with known dynamics to expose implicit strategies ([Nagel](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34104)); weak signals with R² of 1–5% need 25–30+ years of data for reliable high-dimensional learning, so rolling 12-month windows for high-capacity models are rejected ([Fallahgoul](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03780v3)). López de Prado's 2025 causal protocol reframes the failure as confounder and collider bias rather than p-hacking alone; its seven steps run from ML variable selection through causal discovery and double-ML estimation to resampled backtests and deflated Sharpe ratios ([ADIA Lab](https://www.adialab.ae/research-series/a-protocol-for-causal-factor-investing)). The full protocol is heavy for one engineer; the actionable subset is steps 6–7 plus explicit confounder reasoning when a feature's economic story runs through size, volatility or industry. Reporting for each candidate is fixed: gross and net Sharpe, turnover, transfer coefficient, realized-to-ex-ante risk ratio, factor attribution, DSR, PBO, and the IS versus OOS versus post-cutoff split stated explicitly. Expectations are set at one-quarter to one-half of backtest Sharpe given the 73% median live haircut and the extra penalty on complexity ([Suhonen et al.](https://alphaarchitect.com/?p=36892)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes "what is the best alpha" into "what survives the pipeline." Every frontier technique that looked like a shortcut in 2023–2025, from LLM agents to foundation models to end-to-end learning and RL, has now met a post-cutoff or leakage-safe audit and lost to a gradient-boosted tree over well-processed features, or to buy-and-hold. The durable gains are unglamorous and compounding: residualized targets, multi-week horizons, seed ensembles, horizon-matched risk factors with an alignment term, a square-root cost term that the optimizer can see, partial trading toward an aim, and auction execution measured against a fixed benchmark. Each of these is worth more net Sharpe than any architecture choice, and together they explain why a part-time build should spend its first year on plumbing rather than models.

The second implication is about where an individual's edge can realistically sit. Published signals in US large caps are crowded and decaying, text alpha decays fastest of all, and the data that powers institutional text and alt-data research is priced for institutions. What an individual can still own is process quality: PIT discipline, a trial ledger nobody else audits, a borrow-fee history started early, calibrated costs from real fills, and the patience to run paper trading as a plumbing test rather than an alpha test. If the Phase-1 composite clears a net Sharpe of 0.5 under the hurdles in this plan, the system is already in the upper part of what the cost-inclusive literature says is achievable; the ML and text layers are options on top of that, to be exercised only when they pay for themselves after costs.
